THE DESIGN QUESTION

What becomes possible when a system remembers what the organization already knows?

ParcelTrace grew from an operational problem I observed while working in the Mayor’s Office of Code Enforcement: useful property research could be repeated when a property returned through an enforcement workflow.

I was not a title researcher. I translated that broader operational observation into a product question: could a persistent, verified baseline help professionals focus on what changed instead of repeatedly starting from zero?

Read the full case study →

SEE THE SYSTEM

Show the history. Make the change obvious.

ParcelTrace is designed around the property as a persistent object—not a stack of disconnected cases.

01

Start from what was already verified.

Prior work becomes a reusable baseline with linked evidence. New records are compared against that baseline so researchers can focus on the delta rather than reconstructing the entire history.

02

Make disagreement visible.

Assessor, permit, tax, and extracted records are treated as signals—not silently merged into a single answer. Conflicts remain visible and consequential ambiguity routes back to a person.

THE PRODUCT RESPONSE

One property.
A persistent history.

ParcelTrace organizes prior research, new records, supporting signals, conflicts, and human decisions around the property itself.

01

Reuse what was verified

Preserve prior work as a versioned baseline with linked evidence, so useful research does not disappear when a case closes.

02

Research the delta

Use AI to search, compare, and explain what materially changed instead of starting from zero.

03

Escalate uncertainty

Keep ambiguous identities, complex chains, and consequential decisions with qualified people.

04

Designed to transfer

Design hypothesis: the same persistent-history pattern may transfer to other jurisdictions and regulated property workflows, subject to local rules, data access, and validation.
