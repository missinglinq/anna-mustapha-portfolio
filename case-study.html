FEATURED PROJECT

One operational problem. A human-AI redesign.

This case study began with direct professional observation, then uses public documentation and secondary research to distinguish what is known from what still needs validation.

OBSERVED IN PRACTICE

The problem

While working in municipal government, I observed a recurring operational issue: property research could be repeated when a property returned through an enforcement workflow, even when useful prior research existed.

The design opportunity is not simply “automate title research.” It is to preserve institutional knowledge, identify what changed, and route only the uncertain or complex work to people.

FUTURE VALIDATION

What I would test before implementation

  • Can researchers distinguish “AI found no change” from “legal title is unchanged”?
  • Which conflicts deserve immediate human review?
  • What confidence threshold should force manual verification?
  • Does source-image + transcription review reduce time without increasing errors?
  • Does market context help researchers, supervisors, or a different user group?

TAKEAWAY

The more interesting AI question isn't “Can it replace the researcher?”

It's: what should AI do, what must it show, when should it admit uncertainty, and where should professional judgment take over?
